## 1. Promise

Textual review complete.

The cold open promises a developed home in danger and unexplained crying-obsidian cargo. The opening proper replaces that emergency with a clearing the protagonist chooses because he likes it, then lets him find, tame and name Moss through ordinary survival activity. Mining for iron produces the crying portal without a summons or narrated warning.

The main story fulfils the echo-reality promise through recognisable geography, failed return, practical marker tests, incomplete personal knowledge and a final source frame that creates the mouth beneath the earlier clearing. The script avoids an ordinary Nether adventure, hidden authority, second speaking character and explanatory final speech.

## 3. Character agency

Textual review complete.

The protagonist chooses the clearing for pleasure rather than plot utility. He approaches and tames the wolf, brings Moss into his routines, enters the portal, attempts a return, constructs stronger tests and trusts the Atlas only where it matches evidence.

In R4 he chooses shelter, lets temporary walls become a home, gathers return materials cautiously and leaves Moss according to an established Nether routine. After the fire, he searches through the night before choosing the dangerous first-threshold attempt. The creeper controls the exact fatal interruption, but the protagonist's decision to complete, key and approach the frame creates the climactic situation and delayed route.

## 5. Theme and character change

Textual review complete.

The protagonist begins by choosing a place simply because he wants to stay. In R4 he repeats that capacity under constraint: an intended camp becomes a home while escape is gathered in pieces. This makes the later loss more than failure of a mechanism.

His practical patience is visible in marker tests, measured collecting and baffle sketches. Grief does not erase that history; it causes him to close the page requiring more time. The route succeeds technically but cannot return the lived relationship he wanted. No narration states a moral or implies that death restores Moss.
