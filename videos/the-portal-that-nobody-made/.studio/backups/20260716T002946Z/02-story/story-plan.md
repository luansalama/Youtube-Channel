# Story plan — The Portal That Nobody Made

> This is an unlocked Story proposal. It develops the current direction without recording a creative lock or claiming production validation.

## Development status

The film is designed as a self-contained mystery of approximately 20 minutes. Its plot follows one personal journey through a single, self-consistent history: the protagonist discovers a portal, sees the aftermath of a disaster, travels to a point before his discovery, becomes the portal’s builder, and then lives through the disaster whose incomplete aftermath frightened him.

The working destruction mechanism is a short chain of lava ignition, temporary spruce staging and connected timber supports. It is narratively integrated but remains provisional until its range, timing, fire spread and water response are reproduced in the intended Minecraft version.

## Audience promise

A lone player in a private survival world finds a lit portal that nobody else could have made. Entering it reveals different moments of the same ravine rather than the Nether. The intended pleasure is the gradual conversion of spatial details, construction habits and apparent threats into one readable causal pattern. The mystery resolves through actions shown from opposite sides, while the climax asks whether a meaningful choice remains when an observed outcome cannot be replaced.

The story would disappoint if the portal led to an ordinary adventure, if history were protected by an invisible force, if the narrator knowingly lied, if the wolf survived through an unprepared convenience, or if the fire depended on an arbitrary supernatural event.

## Story in chronological order

1. The protagonist builds a modest timber-and-stone homestead on the west rim of a ravine, tames a wolf and establishes a descending wooden maintenance stair. A contained lava shelf lies below the stair, while an eastern stone drain offers a non-flammable route away from the house. He routinely leaves the wolf seated when mining.
2. Roughly twenty minutes before the discovery seen in the opening, the protagonist’s older travelling self is ejected into the portal cave. The frame does not yet exist. From concealment he sees his younger self and the wolf above, proving that he has reached his own recent past.
3. The older self wants to warn the younger without making him avoid the portal. He takes three red wool blocks from an outdoor supply barrel and begins a route marker from the wolf pen towards the eastern drain. The younger self approaches before an arrowhead can be completed, so the older self hides. This is the unexplained strip the younger self later dismisses as an abandoned marker.
4. The older self recognises the cave’s odd left-wall torch position and asymmetrical footing as details from the portal he entered. He decides to reproduce them. He mines local obsidian from the lava shelf, cools one irregular cobblestone block at the frame’s base and uses a short length of spruce staging to reach the final frame blocks. The staging closes the safe gap between the lava shelf and the existing timber stair. He lights the completed portal and hides.
5. At dusk, the younger self finishes his chores, leaves the wolf sitting and descends. He notices the incomplete red wool line, the reversed torch and the cobblestone footing, but none is decisive alone. He finds the impossible portal and chooses to enter. The older self watches without warning him because he believes the journey supplies the only foreknowledge capable of saving anything.
6. The younger self arrives at dawn after the homestead has burned. The geography is unchanged, the wolf pen is empty and a lone figure crosses the eastern ridge. The figure performs the protagonist’s habitual two-crouch orientation check and places a torch on the left-facing wall, but distance prevents identification. The younger self chases, loses the figure and returns to the portal.
7. Crossing again ejects the younger self into the pre-discovery cave described above. He becomes the older travelling self who leaves the marker, mines the obsidian and builds the portal. No material is created by the loop: the wool, obsidian and timber all come from local resources with ordinary origins. Only the remembered arrangement of the portal lacks an external first design.
8. After the younger self enters, the older self notices smoke. The proposed Minecraft chain is that the nearby lava ignites his temporary spruce staging, which carries fire to the connected maintenance stair and then to separated fronts beneath the timber house. He breaks burning stair blocks and moves his single water source across the roof, but the spread is already too distributed to preserve the structure.
9. The seated wolf is trapped behind its pen while the exposed yard burns. The protagonist hears it as part of the roof gives way. He stops moving the water from block to block in defence of the house. Instead, he relocates the source to the eastern stone drain, creating a temporary wet approach, reaches the pen, opens it and releases the wolf from its seated state. The wolf follows him through the prepared route while the house continues to burn.
10. At dawn the ruin matches the future image exactly. When the portal flares, the older self understands that his earlier travelling self is arriving. He keeps the wolf below the ridge line, deliberately crosses the visible crest, repeats the two-crouch check and places the left-wall torch. His earlier self sees the silhouette and is drawn back towards the portal. Once that self has crossed into the past, the older self extinguishes the frame. He then leads the wolf away at first light.

## Plot and causality

The active opposition is an information conflict between versions of the same person, intensified by physical time pressure. The younger self wants an explanation and a chance to prevent the ruin. The older self wants to preserve the journey because it produced the knowledge required for the rescue. Neither is controlled by the timeline: the older self repeatedly chooses concealment, incomplete warning and reconstruction because he fears that direct contact would remove the only path he knows can save the wolf.

The main chain is causal rather than coincidental:

- Exclusive authorship makes the portal an intolerable contradiction, so curiosity causes entry.
- Entry reveals the burned home and absent wolf, so apparent total loss causes a second crossing.
- The frame’s absence and the visible younger self prove that the second destination is the past.
- The incomplete marker becomes something the protagonist remembers seeing, proving that intervention can be part of established history.
- That proof, plus his desire to give his younger self foreknowledge, causes him to build the portal voluntarily.
- His hurried construction creates the proposed flammable connection that causes the fire.
- His first attempt to save the building consumes the time and water needed elsewhere, forcing a value choice.
- Redirecting the water and releasing the wolf makes the apparently empty future compatible with survival.
- Concealing the wolf and recreating the silhouette causes his earlier self to complete the journey, closing the information loop.

Removing the portal entry removes the investigation, construction and rescue knowledge. Removing the red marker removes the protagonist’s first proof that his interventions already belong to history. Removing the construction hazard makes the disaster arbitrary. Removing the wolf choice leaves the fixed timeline emotionally inert. Removing the final silhouette breaks the fair identity reveal and the cause of the second crossing.

## Audience presentation

The film follows the protagonist’s personal order rather than world chronology.

- The opening establishes the private world, the wolf, the red wool strip, the timber stair, the lava glow and the protagonist’s confidence in his own authorship.
- The portal scene makes its builder the primary question. Its footing and reversed torch are visible but not uniquely identifying.
- The aftermath confirms matching geography before explaining time. The empty pen supports fear without proving death. The distant figure supports another-player and future-self interpretations.
- The pre-discovery arrival rules out a parallel copy. When the protagonist lays the wool blocks he remembers, the audience can infer a fixed history before he accepts it.
- Mining and construction answer who built the portal through matched physical actions. The reveal precedes the climax so that the ending can resolve value rather than merely information.
- The fire exposes the ordinary cause and reproduces the damage already shown. No second outcome is presented.
- The reverse view of the ridge reveals the hidden wolf and confirms the silhouette’s identity. Earlier images remain true; their meaning changes.

Narration stays with what the protagonist believed at each point. It may say that he thought the wolf was gone or believed he could still prevent the fire. It must not state that the wolf died, pretend not to recognise an action after recognition, or imply that several versions of the disaster occurred.

## Characters and relationships

### The protagonist

He wants to identify the portal’s builder and prevent the destruction of the home he authored. He needs to separate preserving a structure from caring for what made it a home. He fears helplessness and interprets careful observation as a route to control. His practical strategy is to compare persistent blocks, leave markers, reproduce the portal and prepare against the fire. His contradiction is that curiosity begins the journey while caution later makes him conceal decisive information. His limitation is not lack of intelligence but his tendency to protect a complete plan after its purpose has changed. His boundary is tested when the wolf and house cannot both receive his remaining time; he refuses to sacrifice the living companion for recoverable blocks.

His consequential choices are to enter, cross again, avoid confronting his younger self, leave an incomplete warning, build the portal, let his younger self enter, abandon the roof, rescue the wolf, recreate the silhouette and finally extinguish the portal.

### The wolf

The wolf is a companion rather than a symbolic prop. Ordinary routines establish trust before danger: it follows during chores, waits at the pen and responds when released from sitting. It cannot understand the marker, open the gate or choose a safe temporal strategy. Its vulnerability turns an abstract question about causality into a concrete responsibility.

The relationship begins with the protagonist treating the wolf as one secure element within an owned home. It ends with him accepting responsibility for the wolf even after the owned structure is lost. The wolf’s absence from the first aftermath is a limitation of the protagonist’s viewpoint, not evidence of death.

## Setups, payoffs, and continuity

| Setup | Initial reading | Pay-off |
|---|---|---|
| Private single-player authorship | The portal implies an intruder or anomaly | The protagonist physically builds it from local materials |
| Asymmetrical cobblestone footing | An unknown builder’s signature | Water used during hurried obsidian mining creates it |
| Reversed cave torch | Someone navigates differently | The same protagonist places it while approaching from the other temporal direction |
| Three red wool blocks without an arrowhead | Abandoned decoration or old marker | He lays them as an interrupted warning; the route later guides his rescue |
| Timber stair separated from a lava shelf | Background survival geography | Temporary staging closes the gap and permits ordinary fire spread |
| One movable water source | Routine equipment | He must choose whether its next placement protects blocks or opens a route to the wolf |
| Seated wolf and closed pen | Safe domestic routine | The wolf cannot self-evacuate; the protagonist must reach and release it |
| Empty future pen | The wolf may have died | The final reverse angle shows it alive below the ridge |
| Ridge figure’s two crouches and left-wall torch | A knowledgeable stranger | The protagonist deliberately repeats the action at the end |
| Identical burned composition | Foreknowledge might create a better version | The same ruin remains, but its apparent total loss was an incomplete reading |

Continuity must preserve the west-rim homestead, north-wall portal cave, lava shelf, connected timber stair, eastern stone drain and ridge sight line across all states. Portal obsidian, red wool and staging timber have ordinary local origins. The protagonist’s equipment may coexist with its younger state during temporal overlap, but no item may be introduced without an earlier source. The wolf is never shown dead, injured beyond recovery or visible during the first ridge silhouette.

## Ending and meaning

The climax grows from the protagonist’s earlier decisions rather than from the timeline correcting him. He chose the risky construction, chose silence when he could warn his younger self, and initially chose to spend scarce time defending the building. The fire converts those choices into immediate pressure. His decisive action is not accepting passivity: it is reallocating the only useful resource and his remaining time towards the wolf.

The final ruin is unchanged, satisfying the established single-history rule. Its emotional meaning changes because the first visit showed an empty frame, not a complete account. The closing image reverses the opening: instead of leaving the wolf seated beside an intact home while descending alone, the protagonist walks away from the burned home with the wolf following, and the unlit portal stands behind them.

A provisional thematic argument is that agency does not require every fact to be rewritable. A person may be unable to preserve the form of a life yet still choose which responsibility carries forwards. The unexplained origin of the portal’s temporal routing can remain open as a question of authorship, provided it is not used to conceal any cause required for the fire, rescue or identity reveal.

## Evidence and scope still required

Before Story lock, production testing must confirm a deterministic version-specific layout for lava ignition, fire spread, water placement and wolf pathing. If the proposed staging chain cannot be reproduced reliably, its geometry should change while preserving the causal function: the protagonist’s hurried portal construction must create an ordinary, visible hazard that his later value choice cannot erase. No audience test, animatic validation or capture validation is claimed at this stage.
