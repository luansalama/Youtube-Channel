# Production script — The Portal That Nobody Made

> Draft ID: `draft-03`. This revision implements the supplied commented notes within the current unlocked echo-reality proposal. It records no creative lock, table read, mechanics test, animatic result or production validation.

## Status and use

- Editorial target: approximately 20 minutes. Scene timings are planning guides, not measured results or structural quotas.
- `R0` to `R4` are crew labels and are never spoken.
- One creator-voiced, third-person narrator stays close to the protagonist's understanding. The on-screen protagonist does not speak.
- Crying-obsidian portal behaviour is invented story canon. Seamless portal views are planned with the Immersive Portals mod; compatibility and capture behaviour have not been verified for the selected Minecraft version.
- Fire spread, wolf behaviour, creeper blocking, charged-anchor staging and surviving-frame geometry must be tested before capture.

## Continuity keys

- **MATCH PATH** uses the same camera position, lens, path and direction of travel in Scenes 1 and 2. R4 contains the developed homestead and smoke; R0 contains the fresh clearing.
- **MATCH FRAME A** is the pre-mining composition near the end of Scene 2, after Moss has been found, tamed and named. Scene 13 repeats the exact image and narration recording.
- The forked, lightning-shaped granite seam is a visual coordinate, not a lore symbol. Frame it consistently in every echo so viewers can recognise place before the Atlas names the pattern.
- The protagonist's survey journal begins mostly blank, with a stitched corner and three-line ravine symbol. The Weeping Atlas is a weathered counterpart with the same repair, symbol and handwriting.
- The protagonist arrives at the clearing alone. He finds and tames the wolf during Scene 2. The crew name is Moss throughout, but no in-world name appears above him until the name-tag beat.
- Moss's rug is moss-green wool. It grows one square at a time as he edges closer to the hearth; the final square later supplies the proposed fire path without receiving ominous narration.
- Echo mouths use crying obsidian, directional tear-like particles and a low layered sound heard only after discovery. Their centres show seamless destination views rather than opaque fields.
- The conventional Nether portal uses ordinary obsidian and familiar audio.
- The completed R4 frame is the source that projects the R0 receiving mouth. A builder-keyed route targets the first threshold; an unkeyed entrant at R0 continues forwards to R1.
- Moss's R4 fate remains unseen. Show no body, collar drop or death message.
- The falling creeper must have a readable dark route from the ledge glimpsed in Scene 11. Its blast waking the charged echo frame is invented canon, not a claimed vanilla mechanic.

### 2. EXT. R0 CLEARING ABOVE THE RAVINE — MORNING / PASSAGE OF DAYS

*Editorial target: 3:05*

**Visual intent.** Replace the later emergency with an unhurried arrival. Let affection for the place precede utility, then build the protagonist's relationship with Moss through ordinary survival routines.

**Audio intent.** Cut the distant fire to morning wind, leaves and footsteps. Let work sounds carry the montage. Do not use portal sound to draw him towards the ravine.

**Action**

The jump cut keeps the exact **MATCH PATH** camera. Morning replaces night. The developed R4 landscape is now an untouched clearing.

On the same path, the protagonist walks into frame alone with simpler equipment and a mostly blank survey journal.

Title over the matched shot: **THE PORTAL THAT NOBODY MADE**

At the overlook, he lowers his pack. Water catches the morning light below. Birch leaves move over a wide patch of grass. He could continue along the ridge, but instead sits for a moment and watches the clearing.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> He could have kept walking. The light was good, the water was close, and for once nothing asked him to hurry. He liked the place. So he put his pack down.

**EARLY-GAME MONTAGE — SEVERAL MINECRAFT DAYS**

- He cuts oak and birch, makes basic tools and raises a small shelter.
- He draws the clearing and a simple three-line ravine symbol in the stitched journal.
- At night, he clears a zombie from the doorway, a spider from the crop edge and a skeleton from beneath the birches.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> Without armour, the skeleton’s arrows cut deep. If the wolf had not chased it down, the clearing might have become the end of his journey.

The protagonist lowers his tool, approaches slowly and offers bones recovered from the skeleton. Hearts rise; the wolf's collar appears.

The montage continues:

- The wolf follows while the protagonist plants wheat and later leads two cows into a simple pen.
- A scene where they both run from agry bees.
- He waits on the bank while the protagonist fishes, then repeatedly settles on the damp green patch beneath the birches.
- A name tag eventually comes up on the line.
- After gathering enough iron for an anvil, the protagonist glances from the blank tag to the green bank and labels it **MOSS**.
- He uses it on the wolf. The name appears above him for the first time.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> It's official now, eh. Moss is family.

- The shelter gains a bed, chest, furnace and roof.
- Moss waits at the door during rain, occupies useful construction blocks and follows the protagonist on short gathering routes.
- The protagonist records the starter build and their explored paths in his journal.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> The shelter grew around two sets of habits.

Later, the furnace consumes the last iron ore and the current pickaxe is nearly spent. The protagonist packs torches, food, a bucket and the journal for an ordinary mining descent.

At **MATCH FRAME A**, he tightens the pack above the ravine. Moss trots into position beside him.

**NARRATOR (V.O. — RECORDING TO REUSE IN SCENE 13)**

> Below the hill was only another day's work. They went together.

The protagonist starts down. Moss follows once the first ledge is safe.

**Production note.** Save **MATCH FRAME A**, including camera, lens, sun direction, equipment, Moss position and narration take, for Scene 13. Fishing a name tag is a planned capture beat, not a recorded probability test.

### 3. INT. R0 RAVINE CAVE — DAY

*Editorial target: 1:35*

**Visual intent.** Introduce the granite seam as an ordinary route marker before it becomes the shared coordinate across echoes. Make the portal a physical opening whose destination can already be inspected.

**Audio intent.** Begin with mining, water and paws. Introduce the unfamiliar low portal texture only after the frame enters view; it does not cause the discovery.

**Action**

The protagonist descends by water and short block placements. Moss waits at a narrow ledge until the route is safe.

At mining depth, a forked granite seam crosses the grey wall like a lightning stroke. The protagonist sketches its outline beside the shaft depth in his journal, then follows an exposed iron vein beyond it.

Around the next turn stands a complete crying-obsidian frame. Tear-like particles travel consistently towards its far face.

The centre is a seamless view into what first resembles the same cave. Rain falls through an opening on the far side while the stone above the protagonist remains dry.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> It was crying obsidian: a frame he had no recipe for, standing open.

The camera moves laterally. The destination shifts with natural parallax. There is no reflected protagonist. Beyond the frame, the same forked granite seam carries a stream of rainwater.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> Beyond it, rain fell through a ceiling that was dry above his head.

The protagonist circles the frame. He checks the adjoining tunnels, ceiling and floor. Each route dead-ends or returns to his new mine; there is no scaffold, camp or second access shaft.

Moss approaches, then looks back at him.

The protagonist brings Moss beside the threshold and watches the rain for one final moment.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> He had come down for iron. Curiosity took him one step farther.

They cross in one continuous movement. Dry cave audio gives way to rain at the threshold.

**Production note.** The destination view, matched parallax, wolf transfer and continuous crossing depend on unverified mod and capture behaviour. A controlled cut or composite remains acceptable if the geography is preserved.

### 6. INT. R3 ARCHIVE ECHO — DUSK

*Editorial target: 1:55*

**Visual intent.** Let the Atlas organise evidence already earned. Its final instruction remains useful, personal and incomplete rather than becoming a complete cosmology.

**Audio intent.** Quiet the score for readable inserts. The destination view begins stable, then skips and darkens as the mouth fails.

**Action**

He lays the journals together. The repair, shorthand, ravine symbol and arrow-shaped number seven match.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

**ATLAS INSERTS — HOLD EACH LONG ENOUGH TO READ**

5. The warning **BUILDER PRESENT AT IGNITION BECOMES THE KEY.**
6. A keyed arrow towards **FIRST THRESHOLD** and a separate note: **UNKEYED ENTRY: NEXT MOUTH.**

The protagonist compares the chain drawing with his marker sketches and the particles entering the frame.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> He trusted the pages that repeated what his own marks had proved.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> Unkeyed, every arrow went forwards. Next mouth. Never previous.

He turns to the complete-frame diagram.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

His thumb reaches the torn edge of the safety diagram.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

The destination view skips. One layer of portal sound drops out. Gravel collapses into the old surface stair and shifts again when the protagonist attempts to clear it.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

They cross as the destination image contracts around them.

### 12. INT. R4 RAVINE — NIGHT

*Editorial target: 1:00*

**Visual intent.** Reduce the final decision to one image and one remembered sound. Let an ordinary Minecraft hazard interrupt the planned ignition, then continue after the protagonist's death without narration.

**Audio intent.** Remove the score. Use the muffled remembered bark, the creeper landing, its close hiss, the explosion and the delayed return of the portal texture.

**Action**

The protagonist checks the **FIRST THRESHOLD** drawing, the charged anchor and the baffle.

The ravine room tone narrows. A single muffled bark returns in the mix: the same performance heard at the conventional gate in Scene 9, filtered as subjective memory rather than placed anywhere in the cave.

The protagonist pauses. He rests one hand on the wolf figure in the Atlas, closes the book and steps into reach of the frame.

He raises the flint and steel.

A heavy landing sounds directly behind him. A creeper begins its close ignition hiss.

There is barely time for his shoulders to tighten.

Cut to black.

The creeper explodes.

No narration follows his death.

After several seconds, return to a locked exterior plate. Smoke and debris fill the ravine. The crying frame still stands. The protagonist is absent; equipped items are scattered outside the baffle.

The charged anchor gives one brief violet flash inside the smoke.

One tear-like particle crosses the frame. Then another. Purple light gathers along the crying blocks, and the empty centre slowly resolves into a seamless view of the corresponding R0 cave—too late for its builder.

The original low portal texture returns.

**Production note.** The fall, fatal blast, item scatter, frame survival, anchor flash and delayed destination view may require separate controlled plates. No vanilla behaviour is claimed for the echo activation.
