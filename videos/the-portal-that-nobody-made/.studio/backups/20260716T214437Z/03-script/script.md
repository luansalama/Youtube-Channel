# Production script — The Portal That Nobody Made

> Draft ID: `draft-01`. This script follows the current unlocked Story proposal, in which echo realities replace the earlier fixed-timeline direction. It records no creative lock or production validation.

## Status and use

- Editorial target: approximately 20 minutes. Scene timings below are planning guides, not measured results or structural quotas.
- `R0` to `R4` are crew labels and are never spoken.
- One creator-voiced narrator carries the protagonist’s interpretation. The on-screen protagonist does not speak.
- Crying-obsidian portal behaviour is invented story canon. Minecraft fire, wolf and respawn-anchor staging must be tested in the selected edition and version before capture.

## Continuity keys

- Every ravine contains the same lightning-shaped granite seam. It is the first orientation anchor in each echo.
- The protagonist’s survey journal begins mostly blank, with a stitched repair on one corner and a three-line ravine symbol. The Weeping Atlas is a weathered counterpart with the same repair, symbol and handwriting.
- Moss’s rug is moss-green wool. Its final added square sits nearest the open hearth and provides the visible fire path.
- Echo portals use crying obsidian, directional tear-like particles and a low layered hum. The conventional Nether portal used for supply journeys has ordinary obsidian and familiar audio.
- Moss’s R4 fate remains unseen. Show no body, collar drop or death message.

### 1. EXT. R4 RIDGE / HOMESTEAD — NIGHT

*Editorial target: 0:35*

**Visual intent.** Open on a personal emergency before explaining the place. Establish the purple supply cargo, the burning rug and the room Moss should occupy.

**Audio intent.** Wind and hurried footsteps give way to close fire, a water hiss and one restrained narration line. No music until the cut.

**Action**

Night. The protagonist crests the ridge from the direction of a conventional Nether portal. Crying obsidian and glowstone fill his pack and hands.

He sees smoke before he sees the house.

He drops the purple cargo and runs. A storage window breaks outward. He passes recoverable chests without looking at them.

At the shared-room doorway, flame has reached moss-green wool beside the stone fireplace. Moss is not visible.

The protagonist empties his water bucket through the doorway. Steam fills the opening, but burning timber falls across the direct route.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> By the time I saw the smoke, Moss had been alone in it for too long.

The protagonist turns, breaks through the shortest plank wall and reaches into the smoke.

Cut to black before the room is revealed.

**Production note.** Preserve the dropped-material positions, water direction, broken wall and fire state for Scene 10. The fire path is a proposed setup, not a recorded mechanics result.

### 2. EXT. R0 CLEARING ABOVE THE RAVINE — MORNING

*Editorial target: 1:25*

**Visual intent.** Replace panic with ordinary companionship. Establish the exact surface composition that will return after the climax.

**Audio intent.** The fire hiss resolves into morning wind, birds and leaves. Introduce the portal hum so quietly that Moss notices first.

**Action**

Title card over black: **THE PORTAL THAT NOBODY MADE**

Morning. In a locked composition marked **MATCH FRAME A**, the protagonist and Moss enter a green clearing above a dry ravine.

Moss checks the grass and sits at the overlook. The protagonist unfolds a map whose explored edge ends at the ridge. He opens his clean survey journal. Its stitched corner is visible.

He draws the distinctive three-line ravine symbol and plants a temporary surface marker.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> The map stopped at the ridge. Beyond it there were no paths, no torches and no one else with access to the world. It was exactly why I had walked so far.

Moss settles into the best patch of sun.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> Moss sat down before I did. I took it as a vote. We could stay.

A low hum rises beneath them. Moss turns his head. The protagonist listens, then checks the blank area on his map.

He packs the journal and starts down the ravine. Moss follows.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> I had marked the ravine as untouched. The sound underneath it made that mark a lie. Before I built anything above, I wanted to know what was already below.

**Production note.** Save camera position, lens, sun direction, avatar equipment, Moss position and journal pose for Scene 13.

### 3. INT. R0 RAVINE CAVE — DAY

*Editorial target: 1:35*

**Visual intent.** Make the portal an authorship contradiction rather than a generic spectacle. Introduce the granite seam and frame geometry clearly.

**Audio intent.** Let mining and paw sounds recede beneath the unfamiliar portal hum. Keep the score curious rather than threatening.

**Action**

The protagonist descends by water and short block placements. Moss waits at a narrow ledge until the route is safe.

A lightning-shaped granite seam cuts across the cave wall.

Beyond it stands a complete crying-obsidian frame. Violet light fills its centre. Tear-like particles stream consistently towards one face.

The protagonist circles it. He checks the ceiling, floor and adjoining tunnels. There is no access shaft, scaffold, camp, dropped inventory or mining trail.

He compares the coordinates with his journal. This cave lies beyond everything he previously surveyed.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> I expected an old tunnel, perhaps a ruined frame. I found a doorway made from blocks that should not have opened this way.

He touches one crying block, then studies his empty construction record.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> I knew every permanent mark I had left in this world. This was not one of them. No one else played here, yet every block had been placed.

Moss approaches the field, then looks back at the protagonist.

The protagonist could retreat. He looks up towards the distant surface, closes the journal and deliberately removes Moss’s wait command. He stands beside him at the threshold.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> I could have sealed the cave and called the mystery a hazard. Instead, I wanted the only answer the frame seemed willing to give. I would not leave Moss waiting beside a door I did not understand.

They enter together. Violet fills the frame.

**Production note.** If reliable wolf transfer cannot be captured directly, use matched entry and exit plates without showing a continuous mechanical teleport.

### 4. INT./EXT. R1 RAIN ECHO — DAY

*Editorial target: 1:35*

**Visual intent.** Keep the geography recognisable while changing everything grown or weathered around it. Let the failed return arise from a reasonable assumption.

**Audio intent.** Cut abruptly from portal pressure to heavy rain. The same portal hum remains beneath the weather.

**Action**

The protagonist and Moss emerge beside the same lightning-shaped granite seam.

Rainwater pours through a new opening in the cave roof. A waterfall occupies the route that was dry. Dense vegetation covers the surface, and the R0 marker is absent.

The protagonist checks his coordinates twice and overlays a sketch of the granite seam with the one in his journal.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> I expected the Nether. I got rain.

He reaches the surface. The ravine profile and distant tree line match, but their growth, water and damage do not.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> The ravine kept its bones: the same turn, the same scar in the stone. Everything living over those bones had chosen differently. Same numbers. Wrong history.

Back at the portal, the protagonist places one cobblestone block against the left foot of the frame. He draws its position in the journal.

He looks through the field as if looking back along a road.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> A strange door could still have two sides. I had come through it. Going back was the sensible experiment. I left one block to prove where I had stood.

He and Moss re-enter from the opposite direction.

On the same composition, rain vanishes and grey ash drifts through dry air.

### 5. INT./EXT. R2 ASH ECHO TO R3 ARCHIVE ECHO — LATE DAY

*Editorial target: 1:35*

**Visual intent.** Turn confusion into method. The absent marks must be readable without narration.

**Audio intent.** Replace rain with dry wind and distant falling debris. Use a short musical pulse only when the second test confirms the pattern.

**Action**

The protagonist steps out into the Ash Echo. A naturally scorched tree line stands above an exposed ravine shelf.

The granite seam is unchanged. The cobblestone block at the frame’s left foot is gone.

He searches the ground, then checks the matching journal sketch.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> I had walked back through the opening. I had not gone back.

He does not cross immediately. He constructs a more deliberate mark: two cobblestone blocks in an L, with a torch on the outer block. He records the arrangement and the remaining block count in his journal.

Moss waits while the protagonist studies the one-directional crying particles. With supplies diminishing, he chooses another crossing.

The portal carries them to a darker cave fitted with an old survey recess. Again, the L-shaped mark is absent.

The protagonist opens his journal beside the bare frame foot.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> One missing block could have been damage. The second mark had been a test. It was gone too.

He writes a single word beneath his sketches: **ECHOES**.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> These were not worlds changing while I blinked. They were different worlds sharing the same bones, and every attempt to retreat had carried us forwards.

**Production note.** Use identical close framing for both marks and both absences. Do not rely on colour grading alone to distinguish R1, R2 and R3.

### 6. INT. R3 ARCHIVE ECHO — DUSK

*Editorial target: 2:00*

**Visual intent.** Let the Atlas organise evidence the audience has already seen. Its incomplete instructions should create possibility and danger, not omniscient exposition.

**Audio intent.** Quiet the score for readable inserts. The portal hum begins stable, then loses layers as the mouth fails.

**Action**

The survey recess is old but recognisably arranged by someone with the protagonist’s habits: torches on the same side, compact storage and the three-line ravine symbol cut into a desk.

Inside a protected drawer lies a weathered journal. It has the same stitched corner as the protagonist’s book.

He places the two journals together. The shorthand, ravine symbol and peculiar arrow-shaped number seven match.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> The book had my repair on its corner, my mark for a ravine and my hand on pages I had never filled.

**ATLAS INSERTS — HOLD EACH LONG ENOUGH TO READ**

1. Several plans of the same ravine laid over one another.
2. A chain of portal mouths with every unanchored arrow pointing forwards: **NEXT MOUTH. NEVER PREVIOUS.**
3. The phrase **ONE FRAME, MANY MOUTHS.**
4. A complete crying-obsidian frame joined to a respawn anchor, with a keyed arrow towards **FIRST THRESHOLD**.
5. A smaller note beside the receiving mouth: **UNKEYED ENTRY CONTINUES FORWARDS.**
6. The warning **THE HAND THAT DISCHARGES THE ANCHOR BECOMES THE KEY.**
7. A blast baffle and distance diagram whose final measurement has been torn away.

The protagonist compares the Atlas arrows with the particles and his two absent marks.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> The first pages were measurements, not prophecy. An unanchored mouth led to the next echo, never the previous one. I trusted that rule because I had already paid to learn it.

He turns to the full-frame diagram.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> A complete frame could point its builder towards the first threshold. The key changed the builder’s destination. It did not make the far mouth two-way. The rest of the method was still only a possibility, and the page that should have measured the blast was missing.

The portal field thins. One layer of its hum drops out. Gravel falls into the old surface stair and more shifts when the protagonist tries to clear it.

Moss backs towards the frame. The protagonist looks from the blocked stair to the failing field, takes both journals and chooses the remaining route.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> There was no safe road out of that recess, and the only road I knew was dying in front of us. Forwards was not home. It was still a direction.

They cross as the field contracts around them.

**Production note.** The Atlas wording is the maximum required exposition. Additional portal lore should not be added in narration unless an animatic proves the visual chain incomprehensible.

### 7. INT./EXT. R4 HOME ECHO — MORNING

*Editorial target: 1:15*

**Visual intent.** Make arrival feel merciful before revealing that the route has closed. Change the objective from travelling to surviving.

**Audio intent.** Begin with birds and stillness. Let the portal extinguish with a soft collapse rather than an explosion.

**Action**

They emerge into a dry, habitable version of the ravine. The granite seam remains.

Behind them, the projected upper blocks of the mouth fade with the field, leaving only an incomplete set of crying blocks around black air.

The protagonist tries ordinary fire. It burns for a moment and goes out without forming a portal.

He compares the broken mouth with the Atlas’s complete frame. He lacks the crying obsidian, charged anchor and safe cover shown on the page.

At the surface, there is clean water, timber and open ground. He plants a marker directly above the dead mouth.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> This echo was quiet. For one second, that felt like mercy.

He looks from Moss to the supplies remaining in his pack.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> The field had closed. Fire would not wake it. I could chase half an instruction with no shelter, or keep us alive beside the only seam I knew. This time, I chose the place in front of me.

He builds a small shelter before nightfall.

### 8. EXT./INT. R4 HOMESTEAD — PASSAGE OF TIME

*Editorial target: 2:30*

**Visual intent.** Earn the house and Moss’s importance through ordinary shared routines. Build the material cause of the fire in plain sight.

**Audio intent.** Use a restrained musical theme assembled from work sounds: block placement, furnace ticks, crops, rain and Moss’s movement. Leave room for several quiet domestic holds.

**Action — MONTAGE IN REPEATED LOCKED COMPOSITIONS**

- The first shelter gains a floor, then walls, then a compact upper storey.
- Moss repeatedly stands on the exact block the protagonist intends to place. The protagonist waits, then leaves that block until last.
- Moss waits at a bridge while it is made safe, follows across and receives food after minor damage.
- The protagonist builds a stone-backed open fireplace in the shared room.
- Moss chooses the bare floor beside it. The protagonist lays a small moss-green wool rug under him.
- During a storm, Moss goes directly to the rug while thunder sounds outside.
- The protagonist keeps his current survey journal and the weathered Atlas side by side. His own pages fill with echo diagrams and material counts.
- A conventional obsidian Nether portal is built away from the ravine. Across several journeys, the protagonist returns with small amounts of crying obsidian and glowstone.
- At the dead mouth, he measures space for a complete frame and sketches a stone baffle, but does not activate anything.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> At first I called it a camp. Camp meant temporary.

The house gains crops, storage and signs of use.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> Then the crops came in. Moss learnt which doorway opened first. I stopped taking the bed down. We stayed beside the ravine because every answer we had was beneath it. We stayed long enough for the place above it to become ours.

Moss occupies a construction block. The protagonist lowers his tool and waits.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> Moss had a gift for standing exactly where I needed to build. I began leaving that block until last.

At the hearth, Moss lies with his front paws beyond the rug’s edge. The protagonist adds one new square of moss-green wool between the old rug and the warmth.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> He always found the warmest edge of the room. The night before my last supply run, I gave him one more square of rug so he would not have to.

Hold on the new wool square near the open flame. Stone surrounds the hearth, but wool now provides a path towards timber flooring and wall supports.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> The fireplace looked careful: stone behind it, stone beneath it. I saw the stone. I did not count the soft block I had moved closer.

The protagonist checks a material diagram. Only the last crying blocks and glowstone remain uncollected.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> I gathered the Atlas materials slowly. I meant to wake the frame once, from behind enough stone to come home afterwards. By then, home was no longer the place I was trying to escape.

**Production note.** The final rug extension is the causal change that makes the fire legible. Its distance and route into timber require version-specific testing before the set is approved.

### 9. INT. R4 HOMESTEAD / CONVENTIONAL PORTAL — DAY

*Editorial target: 1:10*

**Visual intent.** Make leaving Moss an established safety decision, not neglect. End on the unnoticed material consequence.

**Audio intent.** Keep the domestic theme warm but spare. After the conventional portal closes, isolate the hearth crackle.

**Action**

The protagonist packs for the final supply journey. Moss stands to follow.

He checks the stone hearth, feeds Moss and gives the sit command on the green rug. Moss settles on the newly added square.

The protagonist shows him the prepared food chest, then leaves through the conventional obsidian portal.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> The last trip was supposed to make the dangerous part less dangerous. I needed the final crying blocks and enough glowstone to charge the anchor. After that, I could measure, build cover and decide.

Moss remains beside the hearth.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> He came on every ordinary journey. The Nether was not ordinary. I sat him in the room he knew, away from cliffs, ghasts and lava. Everything I understood said the house was safer than the trip.

The conventional portal sound fades.

Hold on the open hearth and the nearest wool square. Fire appears on its edge. Moss remains sitting as the first flame spreads out of frame.

Cut before the room is engulfed.

**Production note.** Do not imply this spread has been verified. If the selected version cannot reproduce the planned wool-to-timber chain, revise the geometry with human approval while preserving the visible domestic cause.

### 10. EXT./INT. R4 HOMESTEAD AND SEARCH ROUTES — NIGHT TO DAWN

*Editorial target: 2:25*

**Visual intent.** Continue the cold open exactly, then replace withheld fear with an empty space rather than a graphic answer. Let grief accelerate the portal decision.

**Audio intent.** Repeat the cold-open sound mix until the wall breaks. Afterwards, suppress music and use distant fire, footsteps and pauses for possible returning paws.

**Action**

Repeat the cold-open approach in matching shots: purple cargo, smoke, dropped materials, ignored storage, water through the doorway and the wall breaking.

This time, the camera follows through the breach.

The shared room is empty. The stone fireplace remains, but the green rug has burned away. A charred fan leads from its position into the timber floor and wall. Moss’s sitting place is bare.

Show no body, collar drop or message.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> The hearth was still standing. The rug was not. The burn began where I had added the last square and climbed from there into the house.

The protagonist moves the dropped crying obsidian and glowstone out of the fire path, then searches.

He checks behind surviving furniture, the crop field, the ridge, the conventional portal shelter and the ravine route. He carries Moss’s food and stops repeatedly to listen.

At the storm shelter beneath the window, he finds nothing.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> I searched every place he used when thunder frightened him. The crops. The ridge. The path down to the ravine. I carried his food and waited for footsteps.

A long still hold. Only fire and morning birds answer.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> No footsteps came. There was no collar, no message, nothing I could call proof. There was only the empty place where I had told him to stay.

At dawn, the protagonist returns to the ruins. He sees the recovered crying obsidian beside the weathered Atlas.

He opens the page marked **FIRST THRESHOLD**. A small drawing of Moss appears beside his clean R0 journal sketch.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> The Atlas did not promise my way back. It pointed to the first threshold: the last moment I knew Moss was alive.

He looks towards the ravine.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> I could rebuild. I could search farther. I could test the blast from safety. Every careful choice required me to live with not knowing. By morning, I had turned the only fact I wanted into a plan.

He takes the Atlas, his journal, the crying obsidian and the glowstone. The ruined house continues to burn behind him as he descends.

### 11. INT. R4 RAVINE — DAY TO NIGHT

*Editorial target: 1:45*

**Visual intent.** Answer the construction mystery through physical labour and matched geometry. Make the lethal constraint visible before the protagonist chooses it.

**Audio intent.** Build rhythm from block placement and stone work. Introduce the original portal hum only as a faint memory in the score.

**Action**

The protagonist uses the granite seam, surface marker and coordinates to locate the dead mouth exactly.

He fills its missing positions and constructs a complete crying-obsidian frame. Its dimensions and block imperfections match the R0 portal.

He places a respawn anchor according to the Atlas diagram and charges it with glowstone. The frame remains dark.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> The ravine had not moved. The granite scar put me at the same coordinates where the last mouth died.

He places the final crying block.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> Every block had an ordinary origin. I had mined, traded and carried them. The impossible part was only where the completed doorway might appear.

**ATLAS INSERT**

The keyed arrow runs from the full frame to **FIRST THRESHOLD**. At the far mouth, the unkeyed arrow continues into the ordinary forward chain.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> One complete frame here would project the mouth I remembered finding. From this side, the builder’s key pointed towards the beginning. From the far side, my earlier hand would still be unkeyed and carried forwards.

The protagonist builds a thick stone baffle offset from the anchor. He tests whether his arm can reach the activation face. More distance puts it beyond reach; less distance exposes him around the edge.

He opens the torn safety page. The missing measurement aligns with the gap between his cover and the anchor.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> The hand that discharged the anchor became the key. A remote trigger might avoid the blast, but it would not open this route. The Atlas showed cover and distance. Distance was the part it had lost.

He adds the thickest blocks that still permit direct interaction. There is no fully protected position.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> If I stood farther away, I could not be the key. If I stood close enough, I might never use the door.

Night settles above the ravine. The protagonist puts both journals behind the baffle and faces the anchor.

### 12. INT. R4 RAVINE — NIGHT

*Editorial target: 1:10*

**Visual intent.** Resolve the emotional choice before the mechanical outcome. The portal must activate visibly after, not before, the protagonist dies.

**Audio intent.** Remove the score before activation. After the blast, use muffled silence, falling debris and the gradual return of the portal hum.

**Action**

The protagonist looks once at the Atlas drawing of the first threshold, then at the incomplete protection around him.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> I had spent months refusing to wake it until I could survive.

A brief visual memory: Moss sitting in the R0 sun, Moss occupying a construction block, Moss on the rug.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> Then I came home to an empty room and called uncertainty a reason to hurry. Waiting might have saved me. It could not promise to bring him back.

The protagonist reaches around the baffle.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**

> I chose the chance I could touch.

He directly discharges the charged respawn anchor.

The explosion tears through the open side of the baffle. White fills the frame.

From a locked exterior plate, smoke clears enough to show the crying frame still standing, the protagonist absent and his equipped items scattered. He has died in the blast.

No narration follows his death.

For several seconds, nothing happens.

One violet particle draws across the frame. Then another. Purple light gathers between the crying blocks and forms a portal plane a fraction too late.

The original low hum returns.

**Production note.** The blast, item scatter, surviving frame and delayed field may be assembled from separate controlled plates. This scripted outcome is not evidence that the complete effect works in unmodified Minecraft.

### 13. THROUGH THE FRAME / R0 CLEARING — MORNING

*Editorial target: 1:00*

**Visual intent.** Reveal the apparent beginning as the destination. Repetition should transform meaning without adding explanatory dialogue.

**Audio intent.** Carry the portal hum through the transition, then reuse the exact Scene 2 narration take and morning ambience.

**Action**

The camera remains after the protagonist’s death. It enters the active R4 frame as omniscient audience access, not as his viewpoint or surviving consciousness.

Dark stone passes in stylised layers. The camera follows the matching granite seam, rises through corresponding underground space and reaches the receiving mouth in R0.

It continues upwards through soil into **MATCH FRAME A**.

Morning. The earlier protagonist opens his mostly blank survey journal. Its stitched corner and three-line ravine symbol are clear. Moss sits at the overlook.

**NARRATOR (V.O. — IDENTICAL RECORDING FROM SCENE 2)**

> Moss sat down before I did. I took it as a vote. We could stay.

The low portal hum rises from beneath them.

The protagonist pauses and turns towards the ravine.

Cut underground to the complete active crying-obsidian mouth waiting in the cave where nobody in R0 placed it.

Cut to black.

**Production note.** The through-ground move may use a purpose-built vertical set and matched dissolves. It must not imply that the dead protagonist or his consciousness crosses the portal.

**End.**
