## 1. Promise

Textual review complete.

The cold open promises danger to Moss and unexplained purple cargo. The opening proper supplies the impossible crying portal early, and the main body follows travel through recognisable but incompatible versions of the same ravine. The final construction answers who created the connection, while the delayed activation resolves whether the protagonist can use it.

The script avoids an ordinary Nether adventure, hidden authority, second speaking character and late explanatory speech. The current ending is tragic rather than the earlier fixed-timeline rescue ending, in accordance with the supplied Story proposal.

## 3. Character agency

Textual review complete.

The protagonist makes consequential decisions in Scenes 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10 and 12. His investigation, entry, return attempt and tests create knowledge. Choosing shelter creates the home worth losing. Choosing to leave Moss in the normally safer room creates separation during the fire. Choosing immediate direct activation creates the fatal climax.

The echo system constrains destinations but does not force motivation. The final decision remains avoidable: he could rebuild, search longer or test from safety.

## 5. Theme and character change

Textual review complete.

The thematic question has shifted with the unlocked Story plan: technical access to another reality cannot restore the lived relationship that gave the current home meaning. The protagonist begins as a patient observer who verifies evidence. Grief then turns incomplete evidence into permission to hurry.

His final action succeeds mechanically and fails personally. The script does not state a moral or imply that his death restores Moss.

## 12. Audience-results plan

After release, investigate rather than assume:

- **Introduction retention:** Do viewers remain through the cut from the fire to the quiet clearing, and do comments indicate that the transition feels intriguing rather than misleading?
- **First-portal transition:** Is there a dip before entry, during orientation in R1 or after the failed return? Inspect the corresponding edit before attributing a cause.
- **Flat sections:** Do the R2/R3 investigation or domestic montage sustain attention? Compare visual change, narration density and comment references.
- **Gradual decline:** Does decline correlate with repeated geography, Atlas reading time or route terminology? Treat each as a hypothesis, not a graph-derived conclusion.
- **Spikes and rewatches:** Do viewers revisit the missing marks, added rug square, Atlas key diagram, explosion or repeated final composition? Check whether spikes reflect clarity, surprise or external sharing.
- **Dips at reveals:** If viewers leave during the Atlas, fire continuation or final ascent, review information load and shot duration before proposing structural changes.
- **Comment comprehension:** Classify whether viewers understood who created the connection, why R0 entry leads to R1, whether the protagonist died and why Moss’s fate remains unknown.
- **Emotional response:** Separate comments about Moss, the home and the protagonist’s decision from comments reporting confusion.
- **Packaging performance:** Compare title and thumbnail click performance with the retention of viewers they attract. Do not optimise clicks independently of promise fulfilment.

No audience result or causal conclusion is claimed in this draft.
