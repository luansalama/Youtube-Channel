## Timeline

### World chronology

| World time | Objective event |
|---|---|
| W0 — earlier ravine arrival | The travelling protagonist is ejected through a temporary temporal plane into the pristine north-wall cave. No portal frame or cabin exists. His pre-travel self is several days away on a distant solo expedition and never enters this geography during W0–W4. |
| W1 — homestead period | The travelling protagonist camps on the west shelf, obtains bones from a skeleton, tames Moss and builds the spruce-and-cobblestone cabin, protected chest recess and ravine stair. The normal routine is to let Moss follow during safe work, feed it after damage, sit it beside the bed before lava work and release it on return. |
| W2 — recognition and prevention | The protagonist matches his foundation, granite notch, chest recess and stair rib to the burned ruin he remembers. He packs to leave with Moss at dawn, stone-clads the visible timber route and begins cooling the lava shelf. One stripped-spruce support remains continuous behind the cladding. |
| W3 — fire and loss | Mining the final visible cooled block opens a lateral lava flow. It reaches the concealed support and fire travels into the cabin. Moss is still sitting beside the bed. The protagonist directs every rescue action towards the wolf but reaches the interaction after terminal fire damage. Moss dies and the cabin burns to its opening state. |
| W4 — portal construction and death | The protagonist traces the fire, uses the locally mined obsidian to build the exact remembered frame and prepares a lava-and-spruce ignition. Rescue water has removed a torch from a lateral branch. A creeper approaches and kills him. The blast breaks the lava-retaining lip; subsequent lava ignition and fire activate the portal. Ordinary drops are destroyed by lava or fire. |
| W5 — discovery | The pre-travel protagonist reaches the ravine later in world chronology. He finds the burned cabin, blast cavity and active portal, sees no other self, and enters. The portal sends him to W0, where he becomes the travelling protagonist described above. |

### Protagonist’s personal order

P1 W5 burned-ravine discovery → P2 W5 portal entry → P3 W0 pristine ejection → P4 W1 cabin and Moss relationship → P5 W2 recognition and prevention → P6 W3 fire and Moss’s death → P7 W4 portal construction, creeper death and activation.

The film presents a short fragment from P6 before P1, then follows P1–P7. The fragment withholds Moss’s fate through a cut but does not present a different event.

### Version overlap

During W0–W4, the protagonist’s pre-travel version exists elsewhere in the same world on the distant route that will eventually reach the ravine at W5. The versions do not share a scene, line of sight, sound cue, item exchange or direct intervention. No double-avatar image is required.

### Closed causal information

The protagonist remembers the frame and later copies it. Obsidian, cobblestone, spruce, water, bones and equipment have ordinary origins. The physical matter is not bootstrapped. The first external origin of the frame arrangement’s temporal routing remains intentionally open in this proposal. It cannot be expanded into a supernatural agent or paradox-correction force without human approval.

## Relationship and power balance

The relationship begins through ordinary taming rather than instant sentiment. Moss follows on timber and food runs, receives food after damage, interferes with building in recognisable wolf fashion and waits beside the bed only when the protagonist descends towards lava. The protagonist releases Moss as soon as he returns.

The protagonist controls sit, follow and route choice. This is responsible play under normal conditions and the source of vulnerability during the fire. Once Moss is threatened, that vulnerability controls the protagonist’s time and route: he ignores the chest, abandons the structure and moves water solely towards the wolf.

Moss must not be shown in a pen, doghouse, kennel, closed decorative enclosure or separate pet room. The sitting place belongs to the shared cabin interior.

## Locations, geography, travel and blocking

### Fixed layout

- The ravine has a west-rim shelf, a north-wall recessed cave, a narrow stone rib and a recognisable granite seam.
- The cabin occupies the west shelf because it is the only practical level footprint near the absent portal site.
- The lower cobblestone foundation bends around the granite seam, producing an L-shaped surviving shell.
- A protected chest recess is cut into natural stone. It survives visually after the timber upper structure burns.
- The protagonist’s bed and Moss’s normal sitting position are in the shared main room, one short wall above the stair approach.
- A player-built stair descends from the cabin towards the north-wall portal cave.
- A lava shelf lies below the stair. The visible section can be cooled, while a concealed lateral source remains behind the final accessible block.
- One stripped-spruce support runs continuously behind a later stone-clad interruption and connects the lower route to cabin timber.
- A lateral cave branch approaches the portal site from beyond the protagonist’s immediate working area. Rescue water removes its torch before the final construction.

### Required repeated compositions

- SC-02 ruined overlook and SC-06 intact overlook use the same foundation, granite and stair alignment.
- SC-03 opening portal inspection and SC-11 construction use the same cobblestone foot, frame asymmetry, lava lip and plank coordinates.
- SC-01 and SC-09 use the same water placement, ignored chest, wall break and reach towards Moss; SC-09 continues beyond the earlier cut.
- SC-12 portal activation resolves into the SC-03 entering-hand composition without depicting two avatars together.

### Travel rules

- Cabin to lava shelf must be long enough for fire to gain time but short enough that the rescue nearly succeeds.
- The protagonist starts the fire response below the cabin, not beside Moss.
- The pre-travel protagonist remains far outside the ravine during W0–W4 and arrives only after the older version’s death.
- No portal transit carries Moss, material or information other than the protagonist’s memory and ordinary inventory.

## Information continuity

- No other avatar, silhouette, footstep trail, written warning or borrowed voice may imply a second player.
- Opening evidence consists only of persistent blocks, missing blocks, fluids, fire state, map context and absence.
- The cold open may withhold Moss’s fate by cutting before the final damage interval. It may not present an alternate rescue or use false narration.
- Moss never drops a collar, tag or sentimental object. Grief is carried by the empty sitting place and established routine.
- The protagonist must not know the fire’s hidden route before SC-08 or the creeper’s role before SC-11.
- The audience must see the hidden support at least once before ignition without having its danger explained.
- The final activation must show blast, changed retaining geometry, lava access, spruce ignition and portal fire as distinct causal stages.

## Production boundaries and unresolved evidence

The exact edition, version, difficulty, fire-tick settings and Hardcore presentation are not supplied. Before Story lock, testing must confirm:

- lateral lava flow after the chosen obsidian block is removed;
- ignition of the concealed support and spread to separated cabin fronts;
- water collection, direction and torch removal;
- wolf sitting, damage timing and interaction range;
- credible player health, armour and shield state before the creeper blast;
- creeper approach from a mechanically valid dark area;
- blast effects on the retaining lip without destroying the required frame;
- lava ignition of remaining spruce and subsequent fire inside the frame;
- portal persistence until W5;
- ordinary destruction or absence of dropped items.

These are candidate mechanics, not validated behaviours. Geometry may change after testing, but any replacement must preserve the same causal responsibilities and environmental pay-offs.
