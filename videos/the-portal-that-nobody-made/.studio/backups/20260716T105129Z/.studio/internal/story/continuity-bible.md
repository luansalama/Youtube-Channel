## Timeline

### World chronology

| World time | Objective event |
|---|---|
| W0 — established past | The protagonist builds the west-rim homestead, timber maintenance stair, wolf pen and eastern stone drain. He tames the wolf and crafts or acquires his ordinary tools and water bucket. A safe air-and-stone gap separates the lower lava shelf from the timber stair. |
| W1 — shortly before discovery | The travelling protagonist arrives through a transient portal plane at the bare north-wall cave. His younger self is still above. He takes three red wool blocks from the local exterior barrel, lays an incomplete evacuation route, places the reversed cave torch, mines local obsidian and erects the portal. His temporary spruce staging closes the gap between lava and timber. |
| W2 — dusk discovery | The younger protagonist completes chores, seats the wolf, notices the red strip, finds the portal and enters. The older protagonist watches from concealment and chooses silence. |
| W3 — night fire | The proposed ordinary ignition chain carries fire from lava to staging, stair and house. The older protagonist first defends the structure, then redirects water through the eastern drain and releases the wolf. The house burns to the state already observed. |
| W4 — dawn aftermath | The older protagonist and wolf travel below the eastern ridge. The younger travelling protagonist arrives from W2, sees the ruin and glimpses the older silhouette. The older self deliberately recreates the crouch-and-torch clue. The younger self crosses from W4 to W1. After that transit, the older self extinguishes the portal and continues forwards with the wolf. |

### Protagonist’s personal order

P1 W2 discovery → P2 W4 aftermath and silhouette → P3 W1 pre-frame ejection → P4 W1 warning and construction → P5 W2 observation of his own entry → P6 W3 fire and rescue → P7 W4 recreation of the silhouette and departure.

Every event occurs once in world history. Apparent repetition is a later personal version observing or causing an event that an earlier personal version has already witnessed.

### Closed causal information

The protagonist builds the portal because he remembers entering that arrangement. The physical matter is not bootstrapped: obsidian is mined at W1, wool comes from the W1 barrel and spruce comes from local stock. The first external origin of the copied temporal arrangement is intentionally open in this proposal. It must not be extended into an explanation that changes the established route without human approval.

The protagonist may narrate his beliefs retrospectively, but each statement must remain true to what his on-screen self understood at that scene. Fear is not evidence: he may think the wolf is gone, but he may not state that it died.

## Relationships and power balance

The protagonist initially has practical power over the wolf: he commands sitting and following, controls the gate and chooses routes. This routine protects the wolf under normal conditions but creates its fire vulnerability. During the climax, that vulnerability gains decision-making power because the protagonist cannot continue treating the house as his only objective.

The relationship changes from ownership inside a controlled space to responsibility carried beyond that space. The final following state must be earned by the release action in SC-11, not by placing the wolf safely in the final shot without showing the causal link.

The protagonist’s opposition with his younger self is intrapersonal but physically staged. The older self knows more about consequences and controls concealment; the younger self controls whether the journey begins. Neither version attacks or coerces the other. The older version influences him through the portal, marker and silhouette.

## Locations, geography, travel, and blocking

### Fixed layout

- The compact timber-and-stone homestead occupies the west rim of the ravine.
- The wolf pen sits on the eastern side of the homestead beside an exposed yard.
- A stone drain or maintenance channel runs from that yard towards a lower eastern escape path. It is non-flammable and visually readable in intact and burned states.
- The timber maintenance stair descends from the west-rim undercroft towards the north-wall portal cave.
- A contained lava shelf lies below the stair. Before W1, a visible air-and-stone gap prevents a continuous flammable route to the stair.
- The portal site is a recessed cave on the north wall, visible from a controlled overlook but not directly from the house.
- The eastern ridge has an upper crest visible from the burned homestead and a lower path that conceals the wolf.

### Travel and blocking rules

- Normal movement from house to portal should feel under one minute; precise capture timing remains a production design decision.
- At W1, the younger self’s route and sight line prevent the older self from reaching the wolf without risking discovery.
- The three wool blocks point from the pen towards the stone drain but lack an arrowhead, permitting dismissal in SC-01.
- SC-04 and SC-12 use the same ridge camera line. The older protagonist is visible on the crest; the wolf remains below its occluding edge.
- Burn damage may remove timber and contents but may not change the ravine profile, portal cave, stone drain or ridge silhouette needed for recognition.

## Inventory, resources, injuries, abilities, and constraints

- Portal frame: locally mined W1 obsidian, enough for the chosen valid frame. No obsidian is taken from the future frame and carried into its own construction.
- Cobblestone footing: locally formed or mined during W1 water-and-lava work; exact geometry must be tested.
- Reversed torch: placed at W1 and still present at W2. The W4 ridge torch is a separate ordinary torch used as a repeated behavioural signature.
- Red wool: three blocks taken from the W1 exterior barrel. The incomplete marker remains through W2 and may be partly lost only during the W3 fire after its rescue-route function is established.
- Spruce staging: local W1 stock. It must visibly close the previously safe gap without making the protagonist’s mistake incomprehensible.
- Water: one carried source used for obsidian work, structural defence, rescue routing and final dousing. Capture tests must show when it is retrieved and moved.
- Tools: diamond pick for obsidian, axe for timber or gate access, flint and steel for portal ignition. Each has an ordinary pre-story origin.
- Armour: equipment condition can help distinguish silhouettes, but no durability break, injury or custom texture may carry an essential clue unless separately established and reproducible.
- Wolf: no fatal injury, unexplained healing or off-screen teleportation. Its escape depends on the SC-11 release and route.
- Time pressure: created by the younger self’s approach, visible fire spread and route closure. It must not be asserted only through narration.

Temporary coexistence between younger and older states of carried equipment is allowed as a consequence of the traveller’s worldline. It does not supply the origin of any construction material.

## Factions, history, public beliefs, and hidden truths

There are no factions, other human players, time authorities or public mythology. The world is private and solitary.

The protagonist’s initial beliefs are that he authored all persistent changes, portals lead to the expected Minecraft destination and foreknowledge should permit a corrected attempt. The hidden truths are that he built the portal, his interventions formed remembered history, his staging caused the fire, the ridge figure is his later self and the wolf survived outside the first viewpoint.

No hidden cosmic actor may be added to force self-consistency. Any later explanation of the anomaly requires a proposed canon change and dependency review.

## Recurring objects, symbols, warnings, lies, and visual motifs

- Portal plane: boundary, curiosity and self-authorship. It changes from unexplained light to deliberately built route to extinguished frame.
- Red wool line: incomplete communication. It begins as discarded visual noise, becomes proof of self-participation and finally functions as a rescue guide.
- Left-wall torch: perspective-dependent habit. It links reverse approaches and the ridge identity without dialogue.
- Two crouches: a compact avatar signature repeated exactly in SC-04 and SC-12.
- Water: first a control tool spread too thin, then a concentrated act of care.
- Timber and stone: recoverable authored form versus durable route and relationship.
- Empty frames: the portal frame and wolf pen both invite false conclusions from absence; later action supplies context.
- Opening/closing movement: the protagonist first walks away from a seated wolf towards the portal; he finally walks away from the portal with the wolf following.

There are no narrator lies. Misinterpretations must be phrased as belief, fear or incomplete observation.

## Open setups and serial threads

- SP-01 through SP-10 have planned resolutions within this film.
- SP-11 is intentionally open: the first origin of the copied temporal routing is not explained.
- The open routing question may not conceal the source of materials, the fire cause, the rescue mechanism, the builder identity or the figure identity.
- No serial continuation is required or promised.
- Exact fire, water and wolf behaviours remain production-evidence questions rather than dramatic mysteries.

## Prohibited contradictions

- Do not introduce branches, resets, alternative outcomes or repeated versions of the fire.
- Do not show the portal in the W1 cave before the protagonist constructs it; only the transient arrival plane may appear.
- Do not source portal obsidian by dismantling the future portal or otherwise create matter without an ordinary origin.
- Do not allow the red wool marker to be complete in SC-01 or absent before SC-06 in world chronology.
- Do not place the wolf in the SC-04 ridge sight line, state that it died, or use remains as false evidence.
- Do not let the wolf escape before the protagonist releases it in SC-11.
- Do not attribute the fire to temporal energy, an unseen villain or arbitrary explosion.
- Do not show timber already connecting the lava shelf to the stair before the W1 staging is added.
- Do not let one water placement visibly cover every fire front and then claim the house cannot be saved.
- Do not reveal the older self directly to the younger self before the ridge silhouette has served its causal function.
- Do not extinguish the W4 portal before the younger travelling self completes the W4-to-W1 transit.
- Do not alter the final burn pattern from the composition shown in SC-03.
- Do not treat the portal’s unexplained temporal origin as permission to change any established rule without review.
- Do not claim the candidate fire chain, wolf route or duplicate capture has been validated until evidence exists.
