# Production plan — The Portal That Nobody Made

> Production development proposal for the authoritative `draft-03` Script. This plan records intended work only; it does not claim a completed build, mechanics test, capture, selected take, technical success, licence approval or creative lock.

## Status and authority

- This plan covers one video in its current Production phase: `The Portal That Nobody Made`.
- `03-script/script.md` controls chronology, scene content, causality, character action, geography, props, sound, VFX and editing.
- Production is organised around exactly thirteen scenes with a combined editorial guide of 20:38. These timings are not quotas.
- Story and Direction artefacts supply broader thematic context only where they agree with the Script.
- Narration may be refined during production, but revisions must not alter action, causality, audience knowledge or established canon. The Scene 2 and Scene 13 mining-departure line must use one identical recording.

## Story-versus-Script conflict resolution

| Conflict | Production decision |
|---|---|
| Story begins R0 with the protagonist and Moss already together | Follow the Script. The protagonist arrives alone, chooses the clearing and begins surviving before meeting the wild wolf. |
| Story uses a surface portal hum to prompt discovery | Follow the Script. The descent is ordinary mining; exposed iron carries him beyond the forked granite seam. Portal audio begins only after the frame is visible. |
| Story repeats the pair arriving and deciding to stay at the ending | Follow the Script. Scene 13 repeats `MATCH FRAME A`, showing the established and named pair beginning the mining descent. |
| Story uses different repeated narration | Reuse exactly: `Below the hill was only another day's work. They went together.` |
| Story requires direct respawn-anchor discharge | Follow the Script. The protagonist charges the anchor, constructs the baffle and raises flint and steel but never completes the intended ignition. |
| Story Atlas language makes direct discharge the key | Use the Script prop wording: `BUILDER PRESENT AT IGNITION BECOMES THE KEY.` Show keyed travel to `FIRST THRESHOLD` and unkeyed travel to `NEXT MOUTH`. |
| Story attributes the final explosion to the protagonist | Follow the Script. A creeper planted on the dark Scene 11 route falls behind him and explodes first. The blast kills him. |
| Story lacks the Script's explicit final-camera safeguard | Scene 13 is omniscient audience presentation. It must not suggest that the protagonist survives, crosses the completed portal or experiences the movement. |
| Older Direction proposes a fixed-timeline older-self builder and a surviving protagonist who rescues Moss | Treat that as superseded development material. Do not build or capture a second protagonist, an older-self reveal or a post-fire rescue ending. |

Moss's fate after the R4 fire remains unresolved on screen. Production must show no body, collar drop, death message or equivalent evidence.

## Continuity controls

### MATCH PATH

- Scenes 1, 2 and 10 use one numerical camera transform, field of view, height, path line and direction of travel.
- R4 carries the developed homestead silhouette, night, smoke and dropped cargo.
- R0 carries the untouched morning clearing, simpler equipment and a lone protagonist.
- Capture Scenes 1 and 10 during one controlled R4 continuity window where practical.

### MATCH FRAME A

- Establish the source frame near the end of Scene 2 only after the wolf has helped, been tamed, been named `MOSS` and become part of the protagonist's routines.
- Record camera transform, field of view, sun direction, weather, equipment, journal condition, pack action and Moss's position when the reference is genuinely created.
- Protect the R0 established-pair world state for Scene 13.
- Use one narration recording in both scenes: `Below the hill was only another day's work. They went together.`

### Granite-seam geography

- The forked lightning-shaped granite seam is a natural visual coordinate rather than a lore symbol.
- Preserve its silhouette, scale and general frame position across R0–R4.
- In R0 the exposed iron vein continues beyond the seam and causes the protagonist's next turn.
- The final R4 frame dimensions and small asymmetries must match the waiting R0 receiving mouth.

### Portal topology and sound

- Intended unkeyed route: `R0 → R1 → R2 → R3 → R4`.
- The completed R4 source opens onto the corresponding R0 cave.
- Entry at R0 remains unkeyed and continues to R1.
- This asymmetric topology requires a dedicated test and must not be assumed from ordinary portal behaviour.
- Crying-frame audio begins underground only after the Scene 3 reveal. It must not attract the protagonist from the surface in Scenes 2 or 13.
- Conventional Nether-portal visuals and audio remain distinct.

### Moss continuity

Maintain separate states for wild wolf, tamed unnamed wolf and named Moss. Record collar colour, name visibility, ownership, health, sit/follow state and position during actual tests and capture. Use short repeatable actions and concealed joins if AI behaviour is unreliable. No R4 post-fire shot may contain a death message, body substitute, collar-like drop or newly recorded bark implying a location.

### Creeper and activation continuity

- The Scene 11 ledge must connect physically to the landing mark behind the protagonist in Scene 12.
- The sub-second green edge receives no sting or protagonist reaction.
- The protagonist charges the anchor and equips flint and steel but does not ignite or discharge the system.
- The creeper explosion kills him before the intended action.
- After death: hold black, return to a protagonist-free plate, show scattered equipped items, one violet anchor flash, initial particles and gradual frame activation.
- The creeper blast waking the charged echo frame is invented story canon and must never be described as vanilla or verified mod behaviour.

### Proof-of-concept gate

Before constructing the full echo chain:

1. Create an isolated profile containing only the candidate stack and required libraries.
2. Record exact sources, filenames, versions and hashes after installation.
3. Create R0 and R1 test worlds from one seed and identical generation settings.
4. Verify the hero ravine, granite seam, cave silhouette and target coordinates.
5. Test R0 dry and R1 rainy through one temporary frame.
6. Record a static view, lateral parallax move, player crossing and Moss crossing.
7. Save, exit, reload and repeat the tests.
8. Add camera, replay, shader and recording tools one at a time.
9. Test one-way behaviour and the asymmetric R4-to-R0 and R0-to-R1 requirement.
10. Select either a live implementation or a documented composite recipe before building R2–R4.

## Production design

- R0: fresh clearing; early shelter; established starter home; `MATCH FRAME A`; active underground receiving mouth.
- R1: rain cave, waterfall, dense surface vegetation and the single-block marker.
- R2: ash drift, scorched tree line, exposed shelf and L-shaped marker.
- R3: compact archive recess, readable Atlas desk and failing mouth.
- R4: fresh arrival, first roof, growing homestead, lived shared room, burning state, burned search state, final-frame chamber and post-blast activation state.

Generate one clean master hero location using one seed, version, generator and generation-affecting configuration. Preserve it untouched and derive every echo from copies or controlled region duplication. Do not use unrelated seeds.

### Modular elements

- Locked `MATCH PATH` ridge and homestead approach.
- R0 clearing, overlook, starter shelter, crops, cow pen, fishing bank and mining-departure marks.
- Master ravine corridor with water descent, granite seam, exposed iron turn and portal chamber.
- Replaceable R1 water and vegetation dressing; R2 ash and scorched dressing; R3 archive recess.

## Capture plan

### Order

1. Master camera and lighting references.
2. R0 fresh arrival and early survival.
3. Wolf intervention, taming, name-tag chain, established routines and `MATCH FRAME A`.
4. R0 mining discovery and portal proof plates.
5. R1–R3 route and marker coverage.
6. R4 arrival, homestead growth and domestic routines.
7. Scene 9 bark, room hold and fire-origin plate from a verified backup.
8. Scenes 1 and 10 during one R4 fire continuity window.
9. Scene 11 construction and creeper-route plant in a separate final-chamber copy.
10. Scene 12 landing, blast, aftermath, flash and activation as controlled plates.
11. Scene 13 frame approach, transition modules and exact Scene 2 repeat.
12. Inserts, ambience and narrowly defined pickups after assembly review.

### Camera and performance

- Record numerical camera, field-of-view, lighting, weather and actor marks when reference plates actually exist.
- Use locked paths for match cuts and destructive composites.
- Preserve lateral movement for portal-parallax evaluation.
- Stage hostile mobs in controlled passes and protect the correct wild, tamed and named wolf states.
- Do not rely on uncontrolled fishing probability for scheduling; the edit must still show that fishing supplies the name tag.
- Capture the creeper route, landing, hiss, blast and aftermath separately if a repeatable continuous take is unsafe or unreliable.
- Do not record direct anchor discharge as usable story action.

### Narration and sound

- Build an animatic with scratch narration before the final voice session.
- Narration refinements may improve clarity and rhythm but may not introduce a surface hum, alter causality, resolve Moss's fate or imply survival.
- Record the Scene 2 mining-departure sentence once and reuse that exact asset in Scene 13.
- Reuse the Scene 9 bark performance in Scene 12 with subjective filtering.
- No narration follows the protagonist's death.
- Keep conventional and echo portal sound on separate stems.

### VFX and edit

- Use live portal rendering only after the proof of concept passes; otherwise composite matched destination plates with perspective and tracked masks.
- Treat particles, keyed routing, anchor flash and delayed activation as invented effects.
- Construct Scene 12 from controlled black timing, explosion sound, smoke, protagonist-free aftermath, items, violet flash, particles and a gradually resolving R0 view.
- The Scene 13 ascent may use matched dissolves through modular layers rather than a literal continuous camera path.

### Naming and backups

No captured filename currently exists. After a file has genuinely been created, use the pattern `<slug>_<scene>_<shot>_<setup>_<take>_<capture-date>.<extension>` and then record its actual name.

For every future session:

- preserve the exact world state used;
- record game, mod, camera and capture settings;
- verify that each recording opens and contains its expected picture and sound before assessing usability;
- retain the working copy and two independent backups where available;
- never overwrite the clean master or sole pre-fire state;
- retain rejected takes until selection and backup review are complete.

A take may be selected only after picture and sound are technically usable, world and character states are correct, matches align, portal or mob action is legible, no unintended interface or death message appears and no forbidden evidence resolves Moss's fate. Selection requires real capture evidence and human review.
