# Story plan — The Portal That Nobody Made

> This is an unlocked Story proposal. It records no creative lock, audience result or production validation.

## Development status

This revision adopts the new echo-reality journey and replaces the previous fixed-timeline tragedy within the unlocked development material.

The protagonist and Moss arrive together at a clearing above a ravine. Beneath it, they discover an active portal made from crying obsidian, although such a frame should not work like an ordinary Minecraft portal. The first crossing confuses the protagonist. After exploring the same ravine in a changed reality, he turns back expecting the portal to undo the trip. Instead, it advances him and Moss into another version of the same place. Further crossings reveal that the gate is a one-way chain rather than a reversible door.

A damaged version of the protagonist’s own survey journal, found in an abandoned echo, explains enough of the invented portal behaviour to support the climax. When the chain finally strands them in a habitable reality, they build a home beside the ravine. A wool rug placed too close to an open fireplace later catches fire while the protagonist is away collecting the last materials needed for a controlled return. He comes home too late and Moss is gone.

The protagonist uses the journal’s instructions to build a complete crying-obsidian frame in the ravine. He discharges a respawn anchor to wake it, knowing the activation is explosive but believing the frame will open before the blast reaches him. The explosion kills him. The portal lights a fraction too late.

The camera enters the active frame, rises through the corresponding underground reality and breaks through the surface into the exact scene presented after the cold open: the protagonist and Moss first reaching the clearing and deciding to stay. The portal he will soon discover already waits below them. The ending therefore closes the physical, emotional and presentational loop without claiming that the protagonist survived the blast.

The portal rules, fire geometry, target Minecraft edition and explosion staging remain proposed until human approval and reproducibility testing.

## Audience promise

A lone player and his wolf discover a portal nobody could have built in an untouched part of their private world. Crossing it does not take them to the Nether. It carries them through increasingly unsettling realities of the same ravine, and trying to go back only moves them farther from the world they knew.

The intended pleasure is cumulative recognition:

- the same geography acquiring incompatible histories;
- a return attempt becoming another departure;
- crude marks proving that the realities are distinct rather than changing around him;
- an older version of his own journal turning an abstract anomaly into a dangerous possibility;
- a comfortable household detail becoming the fire’s ordinary cause;
- the crying frame at the end becoming the impossible portal from the beginning;
- the final ascent revealing that the apparent beginning was always the portal’s destination.

The central questions are:

- Who made the crying-obsidian portal beneath the clearing?
- Why does going back through it lead somewhere new?
- Can the protagonist use what he learns to return to a reality where Moss is still with him?
- Does opening a route matter if he cannot survive long enough to cross it?

The story would break its promise if the realities were interchangeable spectacle, if a hidden wizard or visible second player supplied the explanation, if the rug fire lacked a legible material path, if Moss were forgotten while the house burned, or if the final explosion were presented as a verified vanilla method for activating crying obsidian.

## The selected knowledge source: The Weeping Atlas

The strongest working solution is not a generic ancient lore book. It is a weathered version of the protagonist’s own survey journal, found in the Archive Echo.

At the beginning, the protagonist carries a small field journal in which he records coordinates and draws a distinctive three-line symbol for ravines. In the Archive Echo, he finds an older journal with the same repaired corner, the same shorthand and the same hand. It contains observations apparently made by another version of him:

- several plans of the identical ravine laid over one another;
- a diagram showing that an unanchored crossing follows the next arrow, never the previous one;
- the phrase **ONE FRAME, MANY MOUTHS**;
- a full crying-obsidian frame connected to a respawn anchor;
- an arrow from that frame to **FIRST THRESHOLD**;
- a warning that the hand which discharges the anchor becomes the key;
- a blast-distance page whose final measurement has been torn away.

The book does not explain the universe or introduce a lore authority. Its diagrams record experiments, failed returns and practical observations. The protagonist verifies the one-way rule through his own crossings before trusting the final instruction.

The final surface reveal shows the younger protagonist carrying the clean, mostly blank version of the same journal. This pays off the book’s identity while leaving the exact history of the counterpart who completed it open. That unanswered authorship is reflective residue, not missing information required to understand the fire, the explosion or the loop.

### Reserve approaches if the Atlas proves too explicit

| Option | Dramatic advantage | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| An abandoned echo laboratory | Item frames, marked portals and redstone indicators could demonstrate the rule without prose | Requires a larger set and implies an unseen experimenter without the personal journal pay-off |
| A cartographic palimpsest | Layered maps reveal the same coordinates and crying particles trace a route to the first map | More mysterious and visually elegant, but the respawn-anchor danger may become too obscure |
| A ruined-portal field manual | A concise written book gives the rule directly | Cheapest to stage, but least personal and most dependent on exposition |

The Weeping Atlas is the working choice because it joins information, character identity, visual evidence and the final image without adding another speaking role.

## Proposed echo-portal canon

These are invented story rules, not claims about vanilla Minecraft or real physics.

1. Echoes are stable realities with the same underlying coordinates but divergent histories. They are not resets, dreams or temporary illusions.
2. A crying-obsidian frame binds matching coordinates across echoes. The nearest safe vertical exit may differ, but the ravine profile remains the primary orientation landmark.
3. An unanchored crossing moves forwards through a chain of echoes. Turning round and entering again does not reverse the previous crossing.
4. Blocks placed beside a portal belong only to that reality. A missing cobblestone mark therefore provides fair evidence that the traveller has not returned.
5. A projected mouth can appear in an echo where nobody locally placed the frame. Its material source is the constructed frame in the reality that created the connection.
6. A complete crying-obsidian frame can be keyed to the builder’s first threshold by discharging a charged respawn anchor within it.
7. The builder must trigger that discharge directly. The proposed Overworld discharge is explosive; the frame’s response to it is speculative canon.
8. Activation does not rewrite a reality. It opens a route to another existing echo at the moment bound to the first threshold.
9. The final R4 frame projects the receiving mouth found in R0. The R0 protagonist enters that receiving mouth without a key, so the one-way chain carries him onwards to R1 rather than back to R4.
10. The camera may cross the completed connection after the protagonist dies, but this is audience access rather than evidence that his consciousness survives.

The film should teach only the rules needed for the current decision. Geography, repeated portal composition, adjacent block marks and the Atlas diagrams carry most of the explanation.

## Dramatic opening

### Cold open

At night, the protagonist returns over the ridge carrying crying obsidian and glowstone. His house is already burning. He drops the materials, runs past recoverable storage and empties his water bucket towards the shared room. The wool rug beside the fireplace is aflame. Moss cannot be seen from the doorway.

The protagonist breaks through the shortest wall and reaches into the smoke. The scene cuts before the room is revealed.

A restrained narration line may establish the emotional promise:

**By the time I saw the smoke, Moss had been alone in it for too long.**

The fragment should last about thirty-five seconds. It withholds Moss’s fate but makes the protagonist’s priority unambiguous.

### Return to the beginning

Morning replaces the fire. The protagonist and Moss reach a green clearing above a recognisable ravine. They are already an established pair: Moss follows naturally, waits while the protagonist checks the map and sits at the overlook while he opens his clean survey journal.

The repeated narration line is simple:

**Moss sat down before I did. I took it as a vote. We could stay.**

He draws the ravine symbol, places a temporary marker and hears a low portal hum below. Their decision to investigate produces the first crossing. At this point, the audience reads the scene as an ordinary chronological beginning. The ending later reveals it as the receiving reality created by the final portal.

## Protagonist’s personal journey and audience presentation

1. **Fire without an answer:** The cold open shows the protagonist returning too late to an established home and racing towards an unseen Moss.
2. **A place worth keeping:** Earlier in his experience, he and Moss reach the clearing, mark it in the survey journal and decide to stay.
3. **The portal nobody made:** In the ravine they find a complete, active crying-obsidian frame in an unvisited private world. No path, mine, camp or dropped inventory identifies a builder.
4. **The first crossing:** They enter together and emerge beside the same granite seam in the Rain Echo. The weather, vegetation and water paths are incompatible with the clearing they left.
5. **The failed return:** Confused, the protagonist explores long enough to verify the geography, then re-enters the portal expecting to go home. It sends them to the Ash Echo instead.
6. **A method replaces panic:** He marks the frame’s left foot with cobblestone, records coordinates and crosses once more. The next mouth has no mark. Same place and different histories become the best explanation.
7. **The Weeping Atlas:** In the Archive Echo, an abandoned survey recess contains the older version of his journal. Its diagrams name the one-way behaviour and propose a dangerous method of reaching the first threshold.
8. **The last uncontrolled crossing:** Environmental pressure and a failing portal plane force one more entry. They emerge in a habitable echo. The local mouth closes and cannot be relit with ordinary fire. Without a complete frame, sufficient crying obsidian or a charged anchor, they cannot leave.
9. **A life in one reality:** They remain near the ravine because it is the only known seam between echoes. A shelter becomes a house. The protagonist builds a stone fireplace with an open flame and places a wool rug beside it for Moss. Their routines turn enforced settlement into home.
10. **Preparing a route without abandoning the present:** Over time, the protagonist collects crying obsidian and studies the Atlas. He delays the dangerous activation until he has a full frame, glowstone and a plausible blast shelter.
11. **The absence that completes the cold open:** He leaves Moss sitting on the warm rug during a final supply run through a conventional Nether route. Fire spreads from the open hearth to the wool while he is away. He returns to the cold open, reaches the room and finds Moss gone. He searches the ruins and surrounding paths but receives no response.
12. **Grief turns research into urgency:** The protagonist traces the burn fan back to the missing rug, retrieves the materials he brought home and descends to the ravine. The Atlas’s first-threshold diagram now appears to offer the only reality in which Moss is certainly alive.
13. **The final frame:** He builds a complete crying-obsidian frame at the dead mouth’s coordinates, places and charges a respawn anchor, and constructs an incomplete stone baffle. The torn safety page leaves him with evidence of danger but no reliable distance.
14. **A route opened too late:** He directly discharges the anchor. The explosion kills him before the portal plane forms. Purple light appears through the smoke after his death.
15. **The beginning as destination:** The camera enters, rises through corresponding stone and soil, and reaches the surface in R0. The exact earlier shot continues: the protagonist opens his blank journal, Moss sits at the overlook, and they decide to stay. The portal waits beneath them, completing the loop.

## Reality chronology

| Reality | Distinguishing state | Story function |
|---|---|---|
| R0 — Root Echo | Green clearing, dry ravine, no local player construction above ground; the projected crying portal waits below | The apparent beginning and final destination |
| R1 — Rain Echo | Persistent rain, a new waterfall and dense vegetation, but identical stone and granite profiles | Creates confusion and invites the mistaken return attempt |
| R2 — Ash Echo | A naturally scorched tree line and exposed ravine shelf; the prior cobblestone portal mark is absent | Confirms forward movement into distinct realities |
| R3 — Archive Echo | A small abandoned survey recess contains the Weeping Atlas; portal behaviour becomes unstable | Supplies incomplete but actionable knowledge |
| R4 — Home Echo | Habitable surface, inert local mouth and sufficient resources for long-term survival | Holds the house, wool-rug fire, final frame, death and outbound link to R0 |

The realities need not share a synchronised clock. The final keyed portal selects the R0 moment associated with the protagonist’s first threshold. It does not change an event already shown.

## Plot and causality

The major chain is:

- The clearing offers shelter and the portal hum threatens the protagonist’s confidence that the region is untouched, so he investigates.
- The complete crying frame presents the only direct explanation for its own impossibility, so he enters with Moss rather than leaving the wolf behind.
- The first changed reality confuses him, but repeated geography makes the portal the most credible route home.
- Re-entering advances instead of returning, so he begins marking coordinates and adjacent blocks.
- Missing marks and incompatible world histories establish a chain of echoes, so the Atlas can clarify observed evidence rather than invent the premise late.
- The Atlas and failing mouth force a final uncontrolled crossing, which strands them in R4.
- Staying near the only known seam leads to the house, hearth and wool rug.
- The protagonist’s gradual portal research requires crying obsidian and glowstone, causing the supply journey during which the continuously burning hearth reaches the rug.
- Sitting Moss indoors is an established safety choice for dangerous travel, but it prevents following or teleporting while the protagonist is away.
- The fire and Moss’s disappearance turn a cautious future project into an immediate attempt to reach the first threshold.
- The Atlas’s direct-hand rule requires the protagonist to remain close to the respawn anchor discharge.
- The explosion kills him, but its discharge activates the crying frame.
- The portal’s destination reveals the opening reality and creates the mouth his earlier self will enter.

Removing the failed return removes the central portal rule. Removing the marks makes the reality conclusion arbitrary. Removing the Atlas removes the protagonist’s basis for building and activating the final frame. Removing the domestic Moss scenes makes the rug and loss merely functional. Removing the wool adjacency makes the fire arbitrary. Removing the material-gathering objective removes the reason he is away. Removing the anchor blast breaks the protagonist’s death and the portal’s activation. Removing the repeated surface shot breaks the final loop.

## Characters and relationship

### The protagonist

The protagonist is observant, practical and accustomed to treating Minecraft systems as legible. He begins with healthy curiosity: when the portal contradicts his map, he investigates. His first mistake is understandable rather than foolish. He assumes that a portal can be traversed in reverse because ordinary doors and familiar Nether travel support that expectation.

His strength is evidence gathering. After the failed return, he marks blocks, records coordinates and compares terrain. His limitation emerges gradually: once knowledge offers even a dangerous possibility of reversal, he finds it harder to accept the reality in front of him.

His important decisions are to stay at the clearing, enter with Moss, attempt the return, continue marking echoes, trust the Atlas only after verifying part of it, settle near the dead mouth, build a home, pursue the materials slowly, leave Moss somewhere normally safe, search after the fire, build the crying frame and directly discharge the anchor.

His final choice is not between Moss and the house. The house is already lost. He chooses a lethal chance of reaching a reality with Moss over surviving in a reality where Moss may be gone.

### Moss

Moss is already the protagonist’s tamed companion when the story begins. The relationship is expressed through Minecraft-scale behaviour rather than human reasoning:

- Moss follows while the protagonist surveys the clearing;
- waits while he bridges or enters hazardous areas;
- interrupts construction by occupying useful blocks;
- receives food after ordinary damage;
- sleeps or sits on the wool rug beside the hearth;
- remains home only during the dangerous final supply run.

Moss is not kept in a pen, kennel or separate pet room. The rug is part of the shared living space. Its placement begins as an affectionate domestic choice and becomes the fire’s vulnerable material.

The film confirms that Moss is absent after the fire but does not show a body, invented collar drop or on-screen death message. The protagonist treats that absence as loss. The final R0 image shows an earlier Moss, not proof that the R4 Moss escaped.

### Opposition and pressure

There is no conventional villain. Active pressure comes from incompatible objectives within the protagonist:

- his practical self wants evidence, preparation and a survivable activation;
- his grief-driven self wants the first available route to a reality where Moss is alive.

The echo system has a one-way advantage. The Atlas is incomplete. The fire has destroyed time, shelter and emotional patience. Direct activation keeps the protagonist inside the blast problem. He could delay, rebuild, seek better protection or live without certainty, but every delay feels like choosing the Moss-less reality.

The costs remain concrete. Careful preparation costs emotional acceptance of the present. Immediate activation costs his life.

## Ending and meaning

The informational climax is the activation of the full crying frame. The protagonist has physically built the portal, supplied the crying obsidian, charged the anchor and triggered the event that projects its earlier mouth. Nobody built the portal in R0, but the protagonist built the connected frame in R4 after he had already found it in his own personal order.

The emotional climax is his decision to wake it before he has a verified way to survive. He is not careless about Moss or distracted by possessions. He has searched, found the rug empty and concluded that the only reality in which Moss is certainly alive lies behind the first threshold. The tragedy is that the method works a fraction too late for its builder.

After the blast, the camera remains. Purple light gathers between the crying blocks. It passes through the frame, travels vertically through dark stone and soil, and emerges into morning.

The exact Return to the beginning composition appears. Moss sits at the overlook. The earlier protagonist opens the blank journal and draws the ravine symbol. The earlier narration repeats:

**Moss sat down before I did. I took it as a vote. We could stay.**

A faint portal hum rises from below. The audience knows they will investigate, enter, attempt to return, cross the echoes, build the house and eventually create the connection beneath them.

The ending argues that reaching another reality is not the same as recovering what was lost. The protagonist succeeds in opening the route but cannot turn that technical success into a second life. Home remains meaningful because he and Moss chose and made it together, even though that choice also begins the loop.

Moss’s exact fate in R4 remains intentionally unseen in this proposal. If Story lock requires explicit death rather than absence, that must be a human decision and the fire blocking should be revised without inventing a non-Minecraft corpse or keepsake.

## Minecraft story engine

### Candidate ordinary mechanics to verify

- A tamed wolf can follow the protagonist through the planned portal blocking or be transferred through controlled capture.
- A sitting wolf remains in place rather than following or teleporting during the supply run.
- An open fireplace source can ignite wool placed within the relevant fire-spread range when fire spread is enabled.
- Fire can continue from the rug into adjacent timber and create the planned house state.
- Crying obsidian and glowstone can be acquired through the chosen survival route in the target edition and version.
- A charged respawn anchor can produce the required Overworld explosion, damage and surviving frame geometry.

These behaviours are production candidates, not claimed test results.

### Invented mechanics

- Crying-obsidian frames can form active portals.
- Such portals connect echo-realities rather than the Nether.
- Unanchored re-entry advances rather than returns.
- A directly discharged respawn anchor can key a full crying frame to the traveller’s first threshold.
- The discharge activates the portal after the explosion.
- A constructed frame can project a receiving mouth into another reality at matching coordinates.

The film must not present those rules as vanilla facts. The Atlas, repeated crossings and distinct visual language establish them as this story’s speculative canon.

## Narration and camera

- One narrator carries the protagonist’s interpretation; no additional spoken role is required.
- The protagonist remains silent on screen.
- The narrator may say what he believed at each stage but cannot call the echoes timelines after evidence supports separate realities.
- The Atlas should be read through diagrams and a few short labels, not a long spoken lecture.
- The cold open withholds Moss’s fate by cutting, not by making a false statement.
- The final through-ground camera is explicitly omniscient audience presentation after the protagonist’s death.
- No visible counterpart, double-avatar conversation or explanatory authority is required.

## Production scope and evidence still required

The working target remains approximately twenty minutes, with clarity taking priority over a fixed duration.

The smallest version worth making requires:

- one ravine reproduced in five strongly differentiated echo states;
- one active crying-frame state, one unstable state, one inert state and one final activation state;
- one compact house in lived, burning and burned conditions;
- one protagonist avatar and one wolf;
- one custom journal prop with a small number of legible pages;
- one conventional supply route for crying obsidian and glowstone;
- one controlled explosion and one stylised vertical portal transition.

Before Story lock, testing or authoritative version-specific evidence is still needed for:

- wolf portal transfer and sit-state continuity;
- fireplace-to-wool ignition distance;
- fire spread from rug to house structure;
- simulation-distance or chunk-loading behaviour during the protagonist’s absence;
- the time available between return and the completed burn state;
- respawn-anchor acquisition, charging, explosion radius and lethal damage;
- frame survival and camera blocking after the explosion;
- separation between verified Minecraft behaviour and the invented activation effect;
- exact repeated coordinates and lenses across all realities;
- readability of the Atlas inserts at the intended viewing size;
- whether Moss’s absence communicates loss without requiring an explicit death image.

No audience test, animatic result, mechanical reproduction, capture validation or final production imagery is claimed.
