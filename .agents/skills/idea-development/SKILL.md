# Idea Development

## Inputs

Channel strategy, production constraints, reusable assets, prior postmortems, and any bounded inspiration analysis.

## Outputs

`data/ideas.csv` entry or `01-direction/direction.md` plus `idea-score.json`.

## Method

1. Define the audience promise as novel situation, human problem, source of pressure, expected pleasure, and disappointment test.
2. Form the premise from disruption, protagonist, difficult goal, central opposition, and meaningful consequence.
3. Record the thematic subject, question, competing values, and provisional argument without turning theme into a sermon.
4. Define protagonist want, need/limitation, fear, strategy, contradiction, boundary, and pressure point; define active opposition with a goal and strategy.
5. Design the ending early: final choice, climax mechanism, emotional result, thematic consequence, final state, and final image.
6. Make one or more Minecraft mechanics, resources, locations, rules, or construction choices causal to goals, reversals, or climax.
7. Produce micro, small, and medium scope variants with cast, locations, creator-hour ceiling, reusable assets, fallback, and kill criteria.
8. Score clarity, hook, visual potential, feasibility, originality, and series potential from 1–5 using the CLI weights. The score compares options; it never approves one.

## Reject when

The experience promised is unclear, the ending is absent, the concept generates only one interesting scene, the protagonist has no consequential choices, Minecraft is replaceable decoration, production depends on unproven giant builds, or the concept reproduces another creator's distinctive work.
